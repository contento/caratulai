# Sagan Profile — Voyager Golden Record

## Overview

The **sagan** profile aestheticizes concepts as if they were data encoded on humanity's most famous artifact: the **Voyager Golden Record** (1977). Gold background, silver lines, minimal geometry, archival precision. The visual language is *cosmic data*—timeless, profound, meant to speak across civilizations.

---

## Historical Context: The Voyager Golden Record

### The Mission

In 1977, NASA launched **Voyager 1** and **Voyager 2** to explore Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune. They carried more than scientific instruments—they carried a message: the **Golden Record**, a phonograph record made of gold-coated copper, containing sounds and images from Earth.

**Purpose:** If either spacecraft ever encountered extraterrestrial intelligence, the record would serve as a greeting and a window into human civilization.

**Key fact:** Voyager 1 has now traveled beyond the heliopause (the boundary of our solar system) and continues transmitting signals. It is the most distant human-made object, carrying the Golden Record into interstellar space.

### The Record's Contents

**Curated by:** Carl Sagan, Ann Druyan, Jon Lomberg, and others at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory.

**What's on it:**

1. **Greetings in 55 languages** — Hello, from Earth
2. **Scientific diagrams** — The human form, DNA double helix, mathematical constants
3. **Music** — Bach, Beethoven, Mozart, Chuck Berry, Blind Willie Johnson, Peruvian pan pipes, Indian classical, Javanese gamelan, Egyptian music
4. **Natural sounds** — Wind, rain, thunder, whale songs, footsteps, laughter, heartbeat
5. **Images** — 116 photographs encoded as audio signals, including: pyramids, great wall of China, human faces, surgical operations, cities, transportation, art

### The Visual Design

The record's cover is **gold-coated copper with engraved diagrams**—instructions for how to decode the record itself. The design is *minimalist scientific*: clean lines, geometric shapes, precise measurements. Every mark is functional. Nothing is wasted.

**The famous graphic:** The pulsar map—a diagram showing Earth's location relative to nearby pulsars, drawn with lines radiating from a central point. It's how an alien civilization could find us.

### Why Gold?

Gold was chosen for:
- **Durability** — will survive billions of years in space
- **Resistance to corrosion** — won't tarnish or degrade
- **Visual significance** — gold represents value, treasure, importance
- **Rarity** — signals that the message comes from a civilization that values this element

The gold/silver color scheme became iconic: **warm, precious, timeless**.

## The Sagan Profile: Translating Record Aesthetics to Visualization

The **sagan** profile asks: *"How would a concept look if it were inscribed on the Voyager Golden Record?"*

### Visual Language

```
Golden background (#d4af37) — precious, rare, cosmic
├─ Silver lines (#c0c0c0) — pathways, connections, data
├─ Geometric shapes — circles, lines, spirals
├─ Precise, minimal — every mark intentional
└─ 48 elements max — archival restraint
```

### Tone & Purpose

The sagan profile generates concepts that feel:
- **Timeless** — meant to be understood 1,000 years from now
- **Archival** — like a message in a bottle, or data inscribed in gold
- **Profound** — suggesting cosmic significance
- **Universal** — intelligible without language or cultural context
- **Scientifically elegant** — form follows function

### Use Cases

- **Cosmic or philosophical concepts** — "universe", "existence", "journey"
- **Fundamental knowledge** — "atom", "DNA", "light"
- **Achievements worth preserving** — "civilization", "love", "music"
- **Messages meant to outlast** — anything you'd want aliens to understand

## Design Notes

### Color Palette

| Color | Hex | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gold | #d4af37 | Background, treasure, cosmic value |
| Silver | #c0c0c0 | Lines, data, transmission |
| Dark gray | #333333 | Accents (optional, sparingly) |

### Element Constraints

- **Max 48 elements** — echoing the minimal data density of the record's diagrams
- **Shapes:** circles, lines, simple polygons, spirals
- **Forbidden:** text, patterns, gradients (keep it archival)
- **Symmetry encouraged** — like the pulsar map's radial symmetry

### Prompt Tone

> *"You are engraving a message on a golden plate for alien civilizations. The concept is [CONCEPT]. Represent it in minimalist geometric diagrams like the Voyager Golden Record: gold background, silver lines, simple shapes, no text. The image should be timeless, comprehensible without language, scientifically elegant. Every mark should be intentional."*

### Composition Guidance

> *"Compose as a technical diagram: central focal point with radiating elements. Use radial symmetry or balanced asymmetry. Primary shape occupies 30-50% of canvas center. Secondary elements orbit or radiate from center. Maintain clear visual hierarchy — one dominant element, supporting elements smaller and peripheral."*

This is injected into the LLM prompt after the tone and before strict rules, shaping *how* elements are spatially arranged rather than just *what* style to use.

## Why This Profile Matters

The **sagan** profile embodies Carl Sagan's vision: that the *most profound truths are often the simplest*, and that *human culture is worth preserving and sharing*. By anchoring visual generation in the Golden Record aesthetic, we inherit its sense of cosmic significance and timelessness.

When you generate a concept in sagan, you're asking: *"If I had to send one image of this concept to an alien civilization, what would it look like?"*
